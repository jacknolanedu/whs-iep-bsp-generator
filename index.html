Meeting Overview

Student Voice — Information from the student

Tip: Try to use the student's own words from your last 1-on-1 chat.

This section is for gathering information directly from the student. Use the guided questions below to ask them and record their responses. Record the student's own words where possible.

If the student was at the meeting — ask them:

  • Is there anything you'd like people to know about how you learn or what helps you?
  • What would you like to get out of this meeting?
Ask the student:
  • What do you enjoy doing? What are you interested in outside school?
  • What subjects or activities do you look forward to at school?
  • What do you like to do when you have free time?
  • What do you think you're good at at school? When do you feel most successful?
  • What do others say you do well? What are you proud of?
Ask the student:
  • What's hard for you at school? What would you like help with?
  • Is there anything that makes learning difficult?
Ask the student (where appropriate):
  • What do you notice in your body or situation before you get frustrated or upset?
  • What could adults look for to help you earlier?
  • Is there something that often happens just before things get hard?
Ask the student:
  • What helps you when things are difficult? What do teachers or others do that works for you?
  • What would you like to keep or try more of? What would make school better for you?
  • How do you learn best — listening, reading, doing hands-on activities, or another way?
  • What makes learning easier for you? When you understand something new, what usually helped?
Record from a school perspective:
  • What supports, adjustments, programs or structures are already in place?
  • What classroom strategies or teacher practices are working?
  • What else the school could offer or strengthen to support this student.

Environments for Success

Ask the student:
  • When do you learn best? What kind of classroom or setup helps you?
  • Do you prefer working alone, with a friend, or in a small group?
  • Do you need quiet, movement breaks, or things to look at (visuals) to focus?

Parent Voice — Information from parent/carer

Record what is going well, what could be improved, and other key information from the parent or carer. This supports a stronger, family-informed BSP and IEP.

Guide the conversation:
  • External supports or services (e.g. therapy, NDIS, paediatrician, tutoring, community programs)
  • Medication, diagnoses, allergies, or other health information the school should know
  • Changes in home situation (e.g. living arrangements, family stress, bereavement, new siblings)
  • What works at home, helpful routines, or preferred ways to communicate with the school

This section appears in the BSP and IEP so the team can use parent voice when implementing and reviewing the plan.

Guide the conversation:
  • How do you see your child finishing secondary school — what would success look like for your family?
  • What goals or hopes do you have for them (learning, confidence, relationships, post-school pathways)?
  • What would you like to see them do or be able to do by the time they leave school?
  • Anything the IEP team should keep in mind so the school plan lines up with your longer-term hopes

Use this to inform the IEP tab — especially the school Long-term goal (optional) and SMART goals — alongside Student Voice, so the year-level direction is holistic (not only academics).
